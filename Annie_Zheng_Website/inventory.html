Inventory & purchasing

Turning menu sales into ingredient demand and a more consistent purchasing workflow.

ContextSweet Bamboo internship
FocusUsage, stock & replenishment
FilesPowerPoint · Excel

The business problem

Biweekly purchasing depended on manual freezer checks and manager judgment. Without a consistent estimate of expected usage, the process was time-consuming and contributed to over-ordering, stockouts and waste.

My contribution

I developed a workflow using emailed MenuSifu POS reports, Google Sheets and Apps Script. Sales are mapped from menu items to dish components, then translated into estimated ingredient requirements and supplier units.

From sales to an order

  • Import daily quantities and check missing dates or duplicate records.
  • Map menu items, sizes and combinations to standard dish counts.
  • Multiply dish counts by ingredient portions and convert usage into supplier units.
  • Compare demand coverage with usable stock and confirmed inbound deliveries.
  • Review exceptions before placing supplier orders.

Original workflow

The inventory workflow was implemented and used by Elmhurst managers. This public workbook is a compact reconstruction using invented inputs.

Public demonstration

  • 14 sales days and five menu items.
  • Three component dishes.
  • Four invented ingredients.
  • 12 linked worksheets.

Production email ingestion and live Apps Script triggers are described rather than embedded in Excel.

The purchasing logic

Use consistent units and an explicit coverage horizon before rounding to supplier cases.

Recipes, pack sizes, stock records and sales inputs are fictional.

Controls and assumptions

Sales-based usage estimates do not capture every portion difference, waste event or receipt error. Physical stock counts remain necessary. Confirmed receipts should be recorded separately from pending supplier deliveries.

Forecast coverage includes the order cycle and lead time. Managers should check shelf life, storage capacity, unusual demand and overrides before ordering.

Measurement framework

  • Stockouts by ingredient and order cycle.
  • Recorded waste cost.
  • Order preparation time.
  • Physical-count variance.
  • Forecast absolute error and WAPE.

Compare matching ingredients and periods. Establish a baseline before setting numerical targets.
