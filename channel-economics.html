Marketplace & channel economics

Comparing channel decisions through their effect on contribution, not just avoided commission.

ContextRestaurant channel economics
FocusScenario modeling
FileExcel

The business question

How could shifting marketplace orders to direct ordering—and changing delivery arrangements—affect costs? Avoided commissions are only part of the answer. Direct orders can introduce provider fees, discounts and incremental delivery expenses.

Analytical approach

The workbook compares 0%, 5%, 10% and 15% sales-shift scenarios while holding total sales constant. It separates commission savings on shifted sales from additional savings on eligible marketplace orders delivered by the restaurant’s own drivers.

Net modeled benefit = avoided commissions + advertising reduction − incremental fees, discounts and delivery costs.

The public demonstration includes additional cost assumptions to make the difference between gross savings and net benefit explicit.

Editable assumptions

  • Share of sales shifted to direct ordering.
  • Platform and own-driver commission rates.
  • Eligible own-driver sales share.
  • Advertising reduction.
  • Direct payment and provider fees.
  • Discounts and incremental delivery costs.

Rates and financial amounts are hypothetical inputs, rather than actual contract terms.

The model isolates the assumed channel and cost changes. It does not forecast customer migration or retention.

Decision rules and limitations

Check whether revenue is recorded gross or net of platform fees before subtracting commissions. Apply own-driver savings only to eligible retained marketplace sales, and include delivery costs incrementally to avoid double counting.

Before scaling a channel initiative, compare actual completed orders and contribution with a baseline or an appropriate comparison group. Validate acquisition costs, repeat behavior and operational capacity.

How to interpret the output

Scenario savings are modeled estimates. A positive result identifies an assumption set worth testing; it is not evidence of achieved savings or a guaranteed sales shift.
